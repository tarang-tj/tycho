About the data

Elevation is real
Lunokhod: LROC NAC DTM NAC_DTM_LUNOKHOD2, 5 m/px native, NASA/GSFC/Arizona State University. Tycho: LROC NAC DTM NAC_DTM_TYCHOPK01, 2 m/px native, resampled to 2.34 m/px for this crop, NASA/GSFC/Arizona State University. Mars (Jezero): USGS CTX DEM M20_JezeroCrater_CTXDEM_20m, 20 m/px, NASA/JPL-Caltech/MSSS/USGS. Chang'e-4: LROC NAC DTM NAC_DTM_CHANGE4, 5 m/px native, NASA/GSFC/Arizona State University. Apollo 17: LROC NAC DTM NAC_DTM_APOLLO17, 5 m/px native, NASA/GSFC/Arizona State University. All public domain / CC0.
Lunokhod 2's parked position is real
25.830°N, 30.914°E, facing southeast with its lid still open, per LROC post 699 "Lunokhod 2 Revisited". The rover model shown there is an illustrative period-accurate silhouette, not a survey model.
Chang'e-4's landing position is real
45.457°S, 177.589°E (±20 m), elevation -5927 m, landed 3 January 2019. Position from LROC post 1087, "Chang'e 4 Lander Coordinates" - the frame that matches the shipped DTM; Liu, B. et al. 2019, "Descent trajectory reconstruction and landing site positioning of Chang'E-4 on the lunar farside", Nature Communications 10:4229, report an LRO-frame value that agrees within ~20 m and is the source for the landing date. (Liu et al.'s other, more-often-quoted coordinate, 45.4446°S 177.5991°E, is in a different reference frame - CE2TMap2015 - not this DTM's; using it here would place the goal about 415 m from the real lander.)
The Apollo 17 LRV's parked position is real
20.1896°N, 30.7769°E, elevation -2628 m (±3.2 m), per LROC's "Spacecraft Related Coordinates - 2016 Update". The astronauts drove it there themselves, with no delay at all; TYCHO drives to it remotely from Earth, 1.28 s behind.
Other landmark models are illustrative
Chang'e-4's lander and Apollo 17's LRV are shown at each level's goal as illustrative type-accurate silhouettes, not survey models. Their position and facing (when known) come from that level's own data files, not invented here; a level without a sourced facing shows its landmark facing south by default.
Chang'e-4's relay delay is an approximate model
Chang'e-4 sits on the lunar far side, with no direct line of sight to Earth, so its signal relays through China's Queqiao satellite at the Earth-Moon L2 point. The one-way delay shown (about 1.71 s) is a simplified collinear estimate (Earth-Moon distance plus Queqiao's distance beyond the Moon, from the NASA Moon Fact Sheet and The Planetary Society's reporting on Queqiao's orbit), not a precise ephemeris: Queqiao's real halo orbit varies roughly 47,000-79,000 km from the Moon (Space: Science & Technology, 2021), and the lander sits about 1,400 km from the sub-L2 point, which changes the delay by only about 2 ms - well inside the halo-orbit uncertainty. See tools/sites/relay.py for the full model and sources.
Surface detail is procedural
Small craters, rocks, and regolith texture near the rover are generated decoration, not measured. Only the large-scale elevation shape comes from the real DEM.
Earth's position is approximate
Shown at its real apparent size and lit consistently with the sun, but its sky position and the geography visible on it are illustrative, not a precise ephemeris.
Mars delay is compressed for play
The real one-way light-time delay is 3 to 22 minutes depending on where Earth and Mars are in their orbits. This game compresses it 15x to 55x (labeled on screen each run) so a sol plan resolves in seconds, not real minutes.
Rover speed is sped up too
TYCHO drives up to 3 m/s so a level fits in minutes. For comparison, Perseverance's top speed is 0.152 km/h (about 0.042 m/s) and Lunokhod's was about 2 km/h.
What you see is the past
Every position, speed, and slope reading on the HUD is delayed telemetry: it already happened by the time it reaches you. The command you send now will not arrive at the rover for another full delay.

A rover on real terrain, at the speed of light

TYCHO

Drive the delay.

TYCHO is a small rover on the real Moon and Mars. Every command you send crosses space at the speed of light, and everything you see has already happened.

Terrain: NASA/GSFC/Arizona State University (LROC) and NASA/JPL-Caltech/MSSS/USGS (CTX DEM). Surroundings beyond the DEM tile are procedural.
